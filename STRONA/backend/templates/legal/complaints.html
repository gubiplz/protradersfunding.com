Complaints Procedure

Version {{ legal.version }} · Last updated: {{ legal.date }}

1. What you can complain about

Anything about the service: an account that was not delivered or does not work as described, a rule you believe was applied wrongly, a breach or a voided result you disagree with, a reward request that was refused or reduced, a refund, a charge, or how we handled your personal data.

2. How to complain

Write to {{ support_email }} from the e-mail address registered on your account, with the subject "Complaint". Tell us:

  • your account login or order number;
  • what happened and when (for a trade or a breach: the instrument and the time);
  • what you are asking for (for example a refund, a correction of a result, a review of a refused reward).

A complaint costs nothing and does not affect your account.

3. How we handle it

  • We confirm in the same e-mail thread that we received the complaint.
  • A member of our team reviews it. Where the complaint concerns a decision made automatically by the risk engine (a breach) or a refused reward, it is reviewed by a person who did not rely on the automatic result alone.
  • We answer in writing, in the same thread, within 14 days of receiving the complaint. The answer says what we decided and why. If we reject the complaint in whole or in part, we give the reasons and the data we relied on (for example the trades, the rule and the recorded values).

4. If you are not satisfied

You can take the matter to court. If you are a consumer, you can also use an out-of-court dispute resolution body in the country where you live{% if legal.adr_body %}; the body competent for us is {{ legal.adr_body }}{% endif %}. Consumers in the European Union can find the body for their country on the European Commission's list of consumer dispute resolution bodies. Using such a body is voluntary for both sides unless the law says otherwise.

For a complaint about personal data, you can also contact the data protection authority, as described in the Privacy Policy.

5. Your statutory rights

This procedure does not limit rights you have under the mandatory consumer law of the country where you live, including rights you have when a service is not in conformity with the contract.
